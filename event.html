Flux & spread rate

White: estimated perimeter-average ROS (m/min). 60 × ΔA / ∫P dt over a 30-minute interval, with time integrated in seconds. Area and perimeter are linearly interpolated between reconstructed arrival times; the perimeter integral uses trapezoids. The initial footprint is a baseline, not growth from zero.

After the first 30 minutes, use the trailing 30-minute window. Earlier timestamps use the first complete 30-minute window when available: this is a retrospective average using later observations, not an instantaneous or real-time estimate. The early plateau represents that same interval. For events with less than 30 minutes of history, use the available interval and draw the estimate dashed. These provisional values have a separate maximum; ROS max includes only complete windows. At least two footprint timestamps are required, and nothing is extrapolated beyond the latest available arrival time.

The H9 perimeter includes all exposed edges and holes. This is average boundary expansion, not head-fire speed. Interpolation assumes gradual changes between observations; it reduces abrupt-step bias but cannot correct cloud gaps, delayed detections, new ignitions or uncertain early footprints. The white curve uses one shared scale for solid and dashed estimates. Click the chart to reopen these definitions.

Purple: mean observed local flux. Bars: peak observed local flux. When both are visible, they use the same linear scale in kW/m². Convert each measurement using FRP(MW) × 0.001 / pixel area(km²). Within each H9 / 10-minute UTC interval, average acquisitions per source, then average sources equally. The chart's mean is area-weighted across occupied H9 cells and then averaged across observed intervals. Missing observations are gaps, never zero. Separate locations are not averaged together before local sensor fusion.

≈ Nominal pixel areas: MTG and MODIS 1 km²; VIIRS 0.140625 km²; GOES and Himawari 4 km². Actual pixel dimensions are used if supplied, but the current STFY stream omits them. Locations are assigned by pixel centre to H9; true footprint overlap, off-nadir pixel growth and cloud/coverage corrections remain unavailable. This is approximate observed radiative flux, not integrated energy.

The white line uses its own linear scale (zero to the labelled maximum); red and blue share the area scale when both are visible. Use the Charts button beside the time slots to toggle bars and curves. A curve shown alone is rescaled to its own maximum. The arrows beside Play focus the selected interval; outward arrows restore the full event. Focus keeps cumulative area and the original ROS history, and hides observations outside the focused interval. Satellite selections rebuild every series. Click the chart to reopen these definitions.
